# From Newton's Apple to Jupiter's Mass

*What moons tell us about their planets, and about themselves*

Mini-project "Moons", **Concepts in Physics and Problem Solving in Physics** (lecturer: Laure Gouba), African Institute for Mathematical Sciences (AIMS) Senegal, November 2026. Group 2.

## The question

Nobody has ever put Jupiter on a scale, yet we know its mass. The deck follows one question: *what can moons tell us about their planets?*

## What the slides show

- For Jupiter's four large moons, distance³ ÷ period² gives the same number. Each planet has its own.
- Newton's Moon test: 60 times farther than an apple, the Moon is pulled 3,600 times less. It falls about 1.4 mm towards the Earth every second, and keeps missing it.
- Kepler's third law turns that number into a mass: M = 4π² r³ / (G T²).
- From Io alone: Jupiter weighs 1.90 × 10²⁷ kg, about 318 Earths. Titan gives Saturn and our Moon gives the Earth, all within 1 % of NASA's values.
- The same idea weighs an asteroid (Ida), Pluto, the Sun and the black hole at the centre of our galaxy.

| Planet (its moon) | Distance | Period | Mass we find | NASA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jupiter (Io) | 421,800 km | 1.77 d | 1.90 × 10²⁷ kg | 1.90 × 10²⁷ kg |
| Saturn (Titan) | 1,221,900 km | 15.95 d | 5.69 × 10²⁶ kg | 5.68 × 10²⁶ kg |
| Earth (Moon) | 384,400 km | 27.32 d | 6.03 × 10²⁴ kg | 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg |

The Earth comes out 1 % heavier: our Moon is heavy enough to add its own mass.
